How ra is trained, from DICOM-derived pixels to SWA weights

ra is one ResNet-50 that reads each knee study twice: once as a bag of 3-slice windows (the global path) and once as zoomed crops around where each finding lives (the crop path). It learns from the public Raptor CoAtNet's predictions instead of the report labels. Each section below covers one stage, with the exact numbers from the code and an interactive part you can play with.

The idea in one paragraph. A whole-knee view is good at "is there effusion" but blurry for "is the medial meniscus torn". So the model also looks at magnified patches where each finding usually shows. Both views share the lower layers of the network (edges, textures, tissue), and each has its own layer 4 and its own head. The training signal is the public Raptor model's opinion (soft probabilities) rather than the report-derived labels: ra is a student copying a stronger teacher.

02The d96 volume: one array per study

Every study is pre-baked into a fixed 96-row stack, the same corpus layout the public 0.949 CoAtNet lineage uses. Training never touches DICOM.

How a row is made

  1. For each of 5 slots pick a series: the first unused series of that plane, preferring the requested fluid-sensitive flag.
  2. Sort its slices by position along the slice normal; pick k slices evenly over 2 %–98 % of the series.
  3. One intensity window per series: 2nd–98th percentile over its picked slices (after the modality LUT; MONOCHROME1 inverted).
  4. Centred square crop of 140 mm (from row spacing), INTER_AREA resize to 384 px → 0.365 mm per pixel.
  5. Empty slot = rows of zeros. mask[row] = row has any non-zero pixel.

03Teacher targets: what ra is told to predict

ra never sees the report labels in training. Each study's 12 targets come from the public Raptor model (nartaa's CoAtNet-2, trained on all competition data plus OAI), run by us on our own d96 cache.

From two models to one target, per finding

order = pct( 0.65·pct(p384) + 0.35·pct(p224) ) values = σ( 0.65·logit(p384) + 0.35·logit(p224) ) target[k-th lowest order] = k-th lowest value weight = 1 on all 12 cells

The public blend averages ranks, and a rank is not a probability. So the target keeps the rank blend's ordering exactly and borrows realistic probability values from the same-weight logit blend.

Try it: eight studies, one finding

Edit the teacher probabilities (illustrative numbers). The ranks are computed over these 8 studies; in the real build they're over all 4,407.

What the targets look like. They're soft: mean entropy 0.538 nats per cell (label-v2: 0.260), and only 5 % of cells are below 0.05 or above 0.95 (label-v2: 54 %). Their order agrees with label-v2's confident cells at AUC 0.974. The teacher saw these very studies in training, so on our training set it's partly recalling its own labels. Fold 0 is scored against label-v2, never against the teacher.

04Picking windows: 16 in training, 94 at evaluation

A "window" is three consecutive rows [c−1, c, c+1] fed to the network as the three colour channels of one image. Centres are drawn over the whole valid range of the 96-row stack, not per series.

The rule (centres())

valid = rows with mask 1 (fewer than 3 → rows 0,1,2) lo, hi = first, last valid row cs = lo+1 … hi−1 (every interior centre) train: split cs into k equal strata, one uniform draw per stratum eval: cs[round(linspace(0, len−1, k))]

Why this shape

Stratified draws guarantee every part of the stack (all 5 series) is sampled each step, while random positions inside each stratum mean the model sees different slices every epoch. With a full stack, 94 evaluation centres cover every interior row exactly once. Interior rows of an empty slot are in range: those windows are blank, and windows at a slot boundary mix two series (try toggling CorNF off).

05Picking crops: where each finding lives

Ten findings get a magnified view. In training the locations come from a pre-computed table made by a different model (MS1). At inference ra finds them itself (section 13).

The MS1 crop table (training only)

MS1 = MedSigLIP-448 fine-tuned on label-v2. Per study and finding it stores its 4 most-attended windows. On each, it stores the centroid of MS1's gradient × activation map (the in-plane point) and MS1's attention weight. MS1 is used only as a train-time teacher of where to look; it never runs at inference.

Per study, per step

  1. For each of the 10 findings draw one of its 4 windows with probability ∝ MS1 attention.
  2. Move its centre by a uniform integer jitter in ±16 px on each axis.
  3. Clip the centre so the box stays inside the 384 px image.
  4. Take rows [S−1, S, S+1] at full 384 px; the box is resampled to 384 × 384 on the GPU, inside the same augmentation as the global view.

So each training study contributes 10 crop images (one per cropped finding, weight 1). Evaluation uses all 4 per finding (40 crops), weighted by the normalised attention.

07ResNet-50, run twice: the shared trunk and the split layer 4

One set of stem–layer3 weights serves both views. Layer 4 is duplicated: backbone.layer4 for windows and layer4_c for crops (initialised as an ImageNet copy, trained at the backbone LR). Pick a pass to see which modules it uses and the tensor shape after each stage.

Dual BatchNorm

All 53 BatchNorms of the backbone (stem 1 + 16 blocks × 3 + 4 downsample) keep two sets of running statistics: one for window images, one for crop images. The learned scale and shift (γ, β) are shared. Zoomed crops have very different pixel statistics from whole slices, and this stops either view from corrupting the other's normalisation. During training each micro-batch normalises with its own batch statistics: 32 window images, or 20 crop images.

08The two heads

Both heads start from 2048-d pooled features, LayerNorm-ed. The global head decides, per finding, which windows to listen to; the crop head scores each crop for its own finding only.

Global head: attention per finding

h_k = LayerNorm(f_k) k = 1..K windows s_k = MLP(h_k) ∈ ℝ¹² 2048→256 tanh, dropout .2, →12 a_kf = softmax_k(s_kf) over windows, per finding f g_f = ⟨ Σ_k a_kf h_k , W_f ⟩ + b_f

Twelve separate soft "spotlights" over the same bag of windows. ACL can attend to central sagittal windows while effusion attends to the suprapatellar region, without any slot or position input: the model infers it from the pixels.

Crop head: one logit per crop

h_c = LayerNorm_l(f_c) c = crops of the study z_c = ⟨ h_c , V_fid(c) ⟩ + v_fid(c) l_f = Σ_{c: fid(c)=f} w_c · z_c train: 1 crop, w=1 eval: 4 crops, w = attention

The crop path has no attention: the crop was already placed where the finding is. It is linear per finding on its own LayerNorm. Effusion and synovitis have no crops, so l = 0 there.

09Loss and the fused readout

BCE(z, y) = softplus(z) − y·z (soft y allowed) L_global = Σ_f w·BCE(g_f, y_f) / Σ w 12 findings L_local = Σ_f w·m_f·BCE(l_f, y_f) / Σ w·m_f m = 1 on the 10 cropped L = 1.0 · L_global + 0.5 · L_local

All weights are 1 for ra's targets. Both heads are trained against the same teacher target; the crop loss has half weight. The readout is never trained directly.

fused_f = σ( ½ (g_f + l_f) ) on the 10 cropped findings fused_f = σ( g_f ) on effusion, synovitis

Averaging logits, not probabilities: if one head is confident and the other unsure, the confident one dominates less than a probability average would let it. The fused head is what's submitted.

Try it: one cell

The minimum of BCE(z, y) is at σ(z) = y, where it equals the target's entropy. A soft target like 0.7 can never be driven to zero loss, which is why ra's train BCE (0.549) sits just above the targets' entropy floor (0.538).

11Learning-rate schedule

Two parameter groups share one schedule: backbone + layer4_c at 1e-4, all head parameters at 1e-3. One epoch of linear warm-up, then cosine down to a 1 % floor. Hover the chart.

 

steps/epoch = ceil(3524 / (2 × 4)) = 441 total = 11,025 warm-up = 441 factor(s) = (s+1)/441 s < 441 = 0.01 + 0.99 · ½(1 + cos(π · (s−441)/(11025−441))) otherwise

After every epoch the model predicts fold 0 (825 studies) and a fixed sample of 300 training studies (the "gap" sample), in eval mode, so train–validation gaps and curves are logged. Weights of epochs 21–25 are saved for SWA.

12SWA and the BatchNorm refresh

Weight averaging

The final weights are the plain mean of the epoch 21–25 checkpoints (every floating-point tensor). In the low-LR tail the model wanders around a minimum; the average sits nearer its centre and usually generalises a little better than any single epoch.

Why a BN refresh is needed

Averaged weights don't match any epoch's BatchNorm running statistics, so the statistics are recomputed from scratch for the averaged network.

  1. Reset every BN's global and crop running stats; set momentum = None (exact cumulative average instead of an exponential one).
  2. Sample 256 training studies (seeded). Model in train mode; no gradients.
  3. Global stats: 16 fixed evenly spaced windows per study at the full 384 px (no 320 centre crop), batch of 2 studies = 32 images.
  4. Crop stats: all 4 crops of each finding (40 per study), placed from the MS1 table, through the crop path.
  5. Save weights_swa21_25.pt (157 MB); predict fold 0 → val_swa_{fused,global,local}.json.

13Inference on Kaggle: the model places its own crops

At test time there is no MS1 table. ra uses its own global attention to decide where to crop. This "self-location" is part of the same forward pass and costs one extra backward through layer-4 features only.

  1. Rebuild the d96 volume from DICOM with the same builder. 94 evaluation windows, 320 px centre crop, fp16 (fp32 retry if any logit is non-finite).
  2. Global pass → layer-4 feature maps [94, 2048, 10, 10], attention a [94, 12], global logits g.
  3. For each cropped finding take its top-4 windows by attention. On each, compute that window's contribution to the finding's logit (a · LN(GAP(map)) · W), backpropagate to the 10 × 10 map, and take the centroid of positive gradient × activation. That gives the crop centre, mapped back to 384 px coordinates.
  4. Cut 4 boxes per finding (128/192 px → 384), run the crop path, weight the 4 crop logits by normalised attention → l.
  5. Submit fused: σ(½(g + l)) on 10 findings, σ(g) on effusion and synovitis.
Train/test placement differs. Training crops follow MS1's table (plus jitter); test crops follow ra's own attention. The jitter and the 1-of-4 draw in training are partly there so the crop path tolerates imperfect placement.

14Details worth knowing

These aren't bugs to fix today. They're properties of the recipe that explain some behaviour you'll see in the numbers.

Resolution differs between training and evaluation

Training globals are 384 px with augmentation (12 × 12 layer-4 grid). Evaluation globals are a 320 px centre crop (10 × 10): same pixel size, 17 % smaller field of view. The BN refresh measures global statistics at 384, then evaluation runs at 320. This is inherited from the public CoAtNet recipe and kept for comparability.

Windows cross series boundaries and include empty rows

Centres are spread over the whole 96-row stack. A window centred on the first or last row of a slot takes one neighbouring row from the next series, and an empty slot in the middle contributes blank windows. The global attention learns to down-weight these.

The teacher is in-sample on our training studies

Raptor was trained on all competition studies, so its predictions on our folds 1–5 partly reflect its own training labels. On fold 0 it is also in-sample, so "fidelity to the teacher" on fold 0 is descriptive only. This is why fold 0 (scored on label-v2) underrated ra: clear6 −0.002 vs v4, yet LB +0.004.

Probability scale vs ranking

Validation BCE against label-v2 is 0.52–0.53 for ra vs 0.387 for v4, because the teacher's values are softer than label-v2. AUC only cares about order, which is why the LB still went up.

Only two regularisers besides augmentation

Dropout 0.2 inside the attention MLP, and weight decay 0.05. No stochastic depth, no feature dropout. SURPASS-F lane F2 tests adding them (drop path 0.2, dropout 0.3 before the heads, stronger augmentation).
