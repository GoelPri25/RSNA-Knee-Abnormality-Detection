J1, layer by layer

J1 is one network with two encoders. A 2.5D ResNet-50 reads 3-slice windows, and a 3D Swin3D-T reads whole MRI series. A fusion head joins them with a single per-finding attention softmax over both branches' tokens. All three parts were trained end to end for 20 epochs on fold 0. This page traces one knee study through every layer, forward and backward, using the exact shapes, learning rates and numbers from scripts/joint_r50_swin_runner.py and the run logs. Every widget is live: change the inputs and the numbers update.

Reading guide. Colours are fixed throughout: ResNet branch Swin branch fusion / heads loss / gradient frozen (no gradient). Shapes are written PyTorch-style, e.g. [32, 256, 96, 96] = batch × channels × height × width. In training, one forward pass holds 2 studies, and 4 forward passes are accumulated into one optimiser step of 8 studies.

1. The whole network on one screen

Click any box to jump to its layers in the explorer (section 3). Arrows show the forward data flow; section 9 replays the same picture with the gradients flowing back.

What a "token" means on each side

ResNet token = one 3-slice window (slices c−1, c, c+1 stacked as RGB), squeezed by ResNet-50 and global average pooling into one 2048-d vector. Training uses 16 windows per study, evaluation 94. The 2D CNN has no idea which window came from where. Order and position are not encoded (R.context is the identity).

Swin token = one cell of the stage-4 grid of one series. Each covers 2 slices × 32 × 32 pixels of the 320-px crop, after 12 layers of windowed 3D self-attention. One series of T slices gives ⌈T/2⌉ × 10 × 10 tokens. A study gives thousands, and each is tagged with a learned series embedding.

The three outputs and the loss

Each branch keeps its own per-finding attention head (deep supervision). Each head produces 12 logits. The fusion head produces a third set of 12 logits from the union of both token sets. All three are trained against the same soft labels (label-v2):

loss = BCE(fused) + 0.5 × ( BCE(ResNet head) + BCE(Swin head) )

Only the fused logits are the model's prediction (val_swa.json). The branch heads exist to keep each encoder learning on its own.

2. Inputs: one study, two views of the same knee

The Both dataset returns, for one study id, the ResNet windows from the d96 cache and the Swin volume from the d96n cache. These are two different preprocessings of the same DICOM series. Labels y [12] (soft, 0–1) and cell weights w [12] come from label-v2 (soft_labels_final_per_target_latD2.json, sha dd0ab9ab…). The data loader uses 8 workers. The GPU then does all augmentation (rr.prepare and s.Prep).

2a. ResNet input: 3-slice windows from a 96-slice stack

The d96 cache resamples every study into exactly 96 slices of 384×384 uint8 pixels covering a 140 mm field. The slices sit in 5 fixed slots: sagittal fat-sat (26), sagittal T1 (22), coronal fat-sat (18), coronal T1 (12), axial (18). A boolean mask marks slices that hold real data. centres() takes the interior of the valid range [lo+1, hi−1] (the whole range from the first to the last valid slice, so slot boundaries and missing slots in between are ignored, as in the public recipe). It then picks window centres c:

  • Training: the interior is split into 16 equal bins, and one random centre is drawn per bin (stratified). Every window set covers the whole stack, but with different slices each epoch.
  • Evaluation: 94 evenly spaced centres (linspace + rounding). If the interior has fewer than 94 slices, some centres repeat. Repeats are harmless because attention pooling just sees duplicate tokens.

Each window is vol[[c−1, c, c+1]], so 3 neighbouring slices become the 3 "colour" channels of an ImageNet ResNet. That is the 2.5D trick: through-plane context enters via the first conv.

2b. Swin input: native series as 3D volumes

The d96n cache keeps the native slices of up to four series per study: sagittal fat-sat, sagittal non-fat-sat, coronal and axial (slots 0–3). It keeps every native slice inside the 2–98% extent, capped at 40 per series, at 384×384. s.Prep turns each series into a tensor [1, 3, T, 320, 320]: grey value / 255, the same value copied into 3 channels (the Kinetics video model expects RGB), normalised with the Kinetics-400 weights' own mean and std. Series are encoded one at a time (batch 1), so each can have its own depth T. Set the depths below. They drive every Swin shape in this page.

Default depths are illustrative. Real studies vary, and the cap is 40 slices. In training, a series of ≥ 16 slices is subsampled to every other slice with p 0.25 ("stride 2"). Each series is dropped with p 0.1, but one is always kept. Tick a box to simulate this for the shapes.

2c. Augmentation lab

All augmentation runs on the GPU after loading. The two branches use different policies, and both draw their own random numbers. The only thing they share is the study. In training, the ResNet's mirror flag and the Swin's mirror flag are drawn independently (one per branch per study). 4. Inside the ResNet branch (Exp 1e recipe)

The branch is timm resnet50.tv_in1k (torchvision ImageNet weights) with its classifier removed (num_classes=0), so it ends in global average pooling to 2048 numbers. It is a plain 2D CNN. All 32 windows of a training forward pass (2 studies × 16) run through it as one batch of 32 independent images. The CNN does not know that windows 0–15 belong to one knee and 16–31 to another. Only the heads regroup them per study (.view(b, K, 2048)).

How information is transformed, stage by stage

Theoretical receptive field (RF) along the main path, in input pixels. At 384 px over 140 mm, one pixel ≈ 0.36 mm. Each layer-4 cell's RF is larger than the window, but its effective RF is concentrated near its centre.

The bottleneck block: why 1×1 → 3×3 → 1×1

Every one of the 16 blocks does the same thing. A 1×1 conv squeezes channels 4× (e.g. 1024 → 256). That mixes features at each pixel without looking at neighbours. A 3×3 conv works in the cheap squeezed space and is the only place spatial context is gathered. In the first block of layers 2–4 it also carries the stride 2 that halves the grid (torchvision style: the stride is on the 3×3, not the 1×1). A second 1×1 conv expands back 4×. The result is added to the block's input (the skip). The block therefore learns a correction to its input rather than a whole new representation. In the backward pass the skip is a gradient highway: ∂L/∂input receives ∂L/∂output unchanged plus whatever the residual path contributes.

Each conv is followed by BatchNorm. In the first block of each layer, the shapes differ, so the skip itself is a 1×1 conv (stride 2 except in layer 1) + BN, called the downsample path.

BatchNorm has a quirk in this setup. In training, BN normalises each channel with the mean and variance over the 32 windows × H × W positions of the current forward pass. Those statistics mix 2 different knees and 5 different sequence types (fat-sat is dark, T1 is bright fat). Each window's activations therefore depend slightly on the other windows in the batch. The running averages (momentum 0.1) collected this way are discarded at the end. After SWA, every BN layer is reset and re-estimated from 256 training studies (section 13). The ResNet backbone's AdamW group has weight decay 0.05 on every parameter, including BN γ/β (no exclusion list on this side).

5. Inside the Swin branch (Model B2 recipe)

torchvision swin3d_t with Kinetics-400 video weights. A "video" here is an MRI series: time ↔ slice depth. The network has 4 stages of shifted-window 3D self-attention, with depths [2, 2, 6, 2], heads [3, 6, 12, 24] (always 32 dims per head) and widths 96 → 192 → 384 → 768. Patch merging between stages halves only height and width. Depth is merged once, by the patch embedding (2 slices → 1 token), and never again.

Frozen vs trained

B2 sets freeze = 4. The patch embedding, stage 1, patch-merge 1, stage 2 and patch-merge 2 (1.54 M parameters) have requires_grad = False and run inside torch.no_grad(). Autograd records nothing there and stores no activations, and the Kinetics features pass through unchanged. These modules are also forced into eval() on every model.train() call, so their stochastic depth is off. Stage 3, patch-merge 3, stage 4 and the final norm (26.3 M) are trained with layer-wise learning-rate decay.

Code layout

S.shared = features[:5] = stage 1, PM1, stage 2, PM2, stage 3. S.branch[0] = (PM3, stage 4, norm), a deep copy of the pretrained features[5:7] and norm. B1 had 3 plane-specific branches. B2/J1 uses branches = 1, so every series goes through the same weights and the series identity is added later as an embedding.

One Swin block

x ← x + DropPath( W-MSA( LN₁(x) ) ) # window attention x ← x + DropPath( MLP( LN₂(x) ) ) # 4× expansion, GELU

W-MSA splits the [T′, H′, W′] token grid into non-overlapping 8 × 7 × 7 windows (392 tokens) and does full self-attention inside each window only. Every other block shifts the grid by (4, 3, 3) first, so the next windows straddle the previous boundaries and information leaks across windows. A learned relative position bias (one scalar per head for each of the 15 × 13 × 13 = 2,535 possible 3D offsets) tells attention how far apart two tokens are. Stochastic depth ("DropPath") drops the whole residual branch of a series with probability rising linearly from 0 (block 0) to 0.25 (block 11).

5a. Window explorer

Pick a stage and a block type, then hover over a token to see exactly which tokens it attends to. Hatched cells are zero padding: the grid is padded up to a multiple of the window. In the shifted block, padding is added before the cyclic roll, so windows wrap around the edges. The shift mask (−100 added to the attention logits, about −∞ after softmax) stops tokens that were rolled in from the far side from talking to each other. Padding tokens themselves are not masked (torchvision behaviour). They act as zero inputs whose keys and values are just the qkv bias.

Depth axis (T′ tokens = slice pairs): windows of 8 along depth, shift 4 in shifted blocks. If T′ ≤ 8 the window shrinks to T′ and the depth shift is switched off.

Relative position bias, concretely

For two tokens in a window at 3D offsets (Δt, Δh, Δw), with Δt ∈ [−7, 7] and Δh, Δw ∈ [−6, 6], the attention logit gets table[index(Δ), head] added. The table has shape [2535, heads]: [2535, 12] in stage 3 and [2535, 24] in stage 4. The pretrained table encodes Kinetics motion priors, such as "the token one frame later at the same place matters". Fine-tuning reshapes it into slice-neighbour priors. These tables are trained (in stages 3–4) without weight decay. Their gradient is the sum of ∂L/∂logit over every pair of tokens with that offset, in every window of every series in the step.

Patch merging (between stages)

Concatenate each 2×2 group of neighbouring tokens (in H and W only) into one 4C vector, apply LayerNorm(4C), then a Linear 4C → 2C without bias. The grid halves in H and W, depth T′ stays, and channels double. Backward: the 4C gradient is split back into the 4 source tokens, so each source token gets its own slice of the gradient.

6. The three attention-pooling heads

All three heads in J1 share one design, the "Raptor" head of the public CoAtNet recipe. A study is a bag of tokens hn. For each of the 12 findings separately, the head learns where to look (an attention distribution over the tokens) and what to read (a linear classifier on the attention-weighted average):

e[n, k] = MLP(h_n)[k] MLP = Linear(d,256) → tanh → Dropout(0.2) → Linear(256,12) a[·, k] = softmax_n( e[·, k] ) one distribution over tokens per finding k pooled_k = Σ_n a[n,k] · h_n [12, d] (an attention-weighted mean token per finding) z_k = pooled_k · clsW[k] + clsb[k] one dot product per finding → logit

Because zk is linear in the pooled vector, z_k = Σ_n a[n,k] · v[n,k] + b, where v[n,k] = h_n · clsW[k] is token n's vote for finding k. A head's logit is therefore a weighted average of per-token votes, and the attention decides whose vote counts. This makes the backward pass easy to read: see the lab.

7. Fusion: one softmax over the union of both branches

For each study, both token sets are projected into a common 512-d space, and the fused head runs the same pooling as section 6 over all of them together:

u_R = LN( Linear_2048→512( h_R ) ) + kind[0] [K, 512] K = 16 (train) / 94 (eval) u_S = LN( Linear_768→512 ( h_S ) ) + kind[1] [N, 512] N = Σ_series ⌈T/2⌉·100 u = concat(u_R, u_S) [K+N, 512] prior[n,k] = gate[branch(n), k] − log(count of that branch) a = softmax_n( att(u)[n,k] + prior[n,k] ) [K+N, 12] one distribution per finding z_k = (Σ_n a[n,k] u_n) · clsW[k] + clsb[k] share_k = Σ_{n∈R} a[n,k]

Why −log(count)? Without it, about 16 ResNet tokens compete with about 6,000 Swin tokens. At initialisation, when all scores are about equal, the ResNet branch would get 16/6,016 ≈ 0.3% of the attention. Its share would also change between training (16 windows) and evaluation (94 windows). Subtracting log(count) makes each branch's total mass equal to exp(gate)·mean(exp(score)), which depends on the average score, not the number of tokens. The branches start at 50/50, and the learned gate [2, 12] (initialised at 0) moves the balance per finding. Only gate[R] − gate[S] matters.

Bottom strip: every token's attention weight a[n,k] (ResNet windows on the left, each side on its own vertical scale; Swin tokens are summed per pixel column because each one carries only ~1/N of the mass). The token count N is taken from section 2b.
Branch dropout. In training, each study, with probability 0.15, removes one randomly chosen branch's tokens from the fused pool: 7.5% drop the ResNet tokens, 7.5% drop the Swin tokens. That branch's own head still runs and still gets its auxiliary loss. If a study has no Swin series, the ResNet tokens are always kept. The aim was to stop the fused head from leaning entirely on one branch.
What the fused head turned out to be. One softmax over the union factorises exactly: a[n,k] = m_R,k · (within-ResNet softmax) for n ∈ R, and likewise for S. The fused logit is therefore a gated mixture of two pooled vectors, with no token-to-token interaction between the branches. After training, the ResNet mass mR was nearly constant per finding (study-to-study sd 0.05–0.10). See section 14.

8. Loss

BCE(z, y, w) = Σ_{study, k} w[k] · [ −y log σ(z) − (1−y) log(1−σ(z)) ] / Σ w (over the 2 studies of a forward) loss = BCE(z_fused) + 0.5·BCE(z_ResNet-head) + 0.5·BCE(z_Swin-head); backward on loss / 4 (accumulation)

Labels are soft: a cell can be 0.15 or 0.85, not only 0/1. The gradient with respect to a logit is simply w·(σ(z) − y)/Σw, so the model is pulled toward predicting the label's probability, not toward ±∞. A cell with weight 0 contributes nothing. The normalisation is per forward pass (2 studies). Accumulating 4 passes divided by 4 gives the mean of four 2-study means. The last step of an epoch accumulates only 2 passes (1,762 = 4 × 440 + 2) and is still divided by 4, so it is a half-size step.

9. The backward pass

(loss / 4).backward() runs autograd from the scalar loss back through every recorded operation. Gradients are summed into each parameter's .grad (fp32) across the 4 accumulated forward passes. Two facts shape everything in J1's backward pass:

  1. Several losses reach the same encoder. The ResNet backbone gets gradient from the fused loss (through pr and the fused softmax) and from 0.5 × its own head's loss. The two signals are added at the LayerNorm output hr. The Swin stages 3–4 work the same way through ps and the Swin head.
  2. The frozen Swin stem is a wall. The output of PM2 was produced under no_grad, so it is a constant to autograd. The backward pass stops at the input of stage-3 block 0. No activations of the patch embedding or stages 1–2 were saved, which is a large memory saving at 80×80 resolution.

9b. Backward walk-through, top to bottom

Precision. The forward pass runs under torch.autocast(bfloat16): convs and matmuls are in bf16, while softmaxes are explicitly in fp32 (.float()). LayerNorm runs in fp32 under autocast, and BCE is in fp32. Parameters and their .grad stay fp32, so the backward kernels compute in bf16 and accumulate into fp32 grads. TF32 is enabled for any remaining fp32 matmuls. bf16 has the same exponent range as fp32, so no loss scaling is needed.

10. Optimiser and learning-rate schedule

One AdamW holds every trainable parameter (52,175,592) in groups with their own learning rate and weight decay. The parameter counts below are computed from the architecture, and they match the profile's "params 53.7M, trainable 52.2M".

Layer-wise lr decay inside the Swin branch

17 "units" in depth order (patch embedding, each block, each patch merge, the final norm). Unit i gets 5e-5 × 0.916−i. Units 0–6 are frozen, so their lr is never used. The lowest trained layer (stage-3 block 0) gets 1.94e-5, about 40% of the top. The idea is to change the pretrained lower layers less than the upper ones.

AdamW, the update every parameter gets

g = clip(∇θ) (global L2 norm ≤ 1.0) m ← 0.9·m + 0.1·g ; v ← 0.999·v + 0.001·g² m̂ = m/(1−0.9^t) ; v̂ = v/(1−0.999^t) θ ← θ·(1 − lr·wd) − lr · m̂ / (√v̂ + 1e-8)

Adam divides by the running RMS of each parameter's own gradient, so the step size of every weight is roughly lr regardless of how large its gradient is. The 0.5 auxiliary weight therefore changes the direction mix between the fused and auxiliary signals, not the step length. Weight decay is decoupled: a pure shrink of 0.05 × lr per step toward zero. Clipping uses one global norm over all 52 M parameters. When it triggers, it shrinks the ResNet, Swin and fusion updates by the same factor.

The schedule

441 optimiser steps per epoch (⌈3,524 / 8⌉) × 20 epochs = 8,820 steps. LambdaLR multiplies every group's base lr by a linear warm-up over the first 441 steps, then a cosine from 1 down to a 1% floor. Drag the slider. The dots are the lrs logged at each epoch end in history.json, which confirms the curve.

    13. SWA, BN refresh, evaluation

    Stochastic weight averaging (epochs 16–20)

    At the end of epochs 16, 17, 18, 19 and 20 the full state_dict is saved. The final model is their plain arithmetic mean, for every floating-point tensor: weights, biases, LayerNorm and BN parameters, relative-position tables, gates and embeddings. Integer buffers (BN's num_batches_tracked) are taken from epoch 20. Over these epochs the lr falls from 1.1e-5 to 1e-6 (ResNet backbone), so the five snapshots sit close together in one basin. Averaging moves the weights toward the basin's centre, which usually generalises a little better than any single endpoint. SWA val fused: 0.9055 (12-target) vs 0.9059 at epoch 20.

    BN refresh (ResNet only)

    Averaged weights invalidate the BN running statistics (they belong to no single model). For every BN layer in model.R: reset_running_stats() and momentum = None. Then 256 randomly chosen training studies (seed 2026) are forwarded through the ResNet backbone in train mode, under no_grad, at 384 px, with 16 deterministic evenly spaced windows and no augmentation, 2 studies per batch. With momentum=None, PyTorch keeps an exact cumulative average over all 128 batches. The Swin branch needs no refresh: it has only LayerNorm, which has no running statistics.

    Evaluation (validation, gold, and the Kaggle kernel)

    • model.eval(): dropout off, drop path off, BN uses the refreshed running stats, no branch dropout.
    • ResNet: 94 evenly spaced windows on the centre 320 crop (not resized). Train-time windows were 384 at the same pixel scale, so the CNN sees the same anatomy size but a smaller field.
    • Swin: every series, every slice, centre 320 crop, no stride 2.
    • Batch of 1 study, bf16 autocast, no TTA. Outputs: fused, ResNet-head and Swin-head probabilities + the ResNet share.
    • Kaggle port: fused max |Δp| 0.0064 vs the training-side predictions on 8 parity studies (ResNet in fp16, Swin + fusion in fp32).

    14. What it learned, and why it failed

    AUC on confident validation cells (label ≤ 0.3 or ≥ 0.7, weight > 0), SWA model. "1e" = the standalone ResNet with the same recipe (d40803d0 labels), "B2" = standalone Swin3D, "A" = mean(1e, B2) probabilities, the bar J1 had to beat. Gate = learned gate[R] − gate[S] (log scale).

    Gate sweep: is the routing the problem?

    Shift every study's ResNet-vs-Swin log-odds by d, recompute the fused logit exactly, and score. The learned routing (d = 0) is already at the 10-target optimum. An oracle per-finding gate fit on fold 0 itself reaches only 0.9097, recovering 39% of the gap to avg(lv2, B2). So the problem lies in the features, not the mixing.

    Which branch carries which finding (occlusion of the fused head)

    AUC change when one series or slot is removed from the fused pool. ACL depends on ResNet sagittal fat-sat windows, MCL on the Swin coronal series, and fracture / contusion / effusion partly on the Swin axial series.

    The diagnosis in four sentences

    1. Branch competition starved the ResNet. Its own head lost 0.008 10-target AUC against the same recipe trained alone (label-v2 r50), concentrated in the findings the gate routed to the Swin (MCL 0.751 vs 0.794, fracture). A linear probe confirms the information is in the windows (window-mean probe MCL 0.750 vs 0.745), but J1's ResNet head no longer pools it well. Its MCL attention spread over 50 effective windows vs 34, with less coronal fat-sat mass (0.25 vs 0.34). Once the fused head took MCL from the Swin side, the ResNet received less MCL-specific gradient. Confound: it also trained 20 epochs instead of 25, at 0.5 aux weight.
    2. The fusion is a mixture, not an interaction. One softmax over a union is a per-finding weighted average of two pooled vectors. No token ever looks at another branch's token, so 2D and 3D evidence are combined but never reconciled.
    3. The fold-0 gain was within noise. Fused − mean of own heads: +0.0013 10-target [−0.0011, +0.0037]. Fused − A: −0.0027. All three pre-registered gates FAILED.
    4. The leaderboard punished it. LB 0.930 (submission 57270107), below label-v2's 0.937 and below its own clear6 fit (0.938). A residual of −0.008 is the opposite of the standalone Swin3D's +0.006 / +0.013. The fusion route (F0, J1, J2) is closed.
